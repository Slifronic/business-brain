# Sycamore: origin and the pivot that made it B2B

Date: 2026-07-07 (recording history from Fall 2025 onward; source: Drive doc "De Anza Founders' Forum, Fall 2025", https://docs.google.com/document/d/1wjQyR74No_6_Fsvtk_T_iLXRC3h6jk5zKRc6oiHxmGc)

**Version 1 (Sept 2025, Founders' Forum + PBL):** "Sycamore — Serve Your Community Always (Syca)." A consumer gamification app: event-specific collectible badges, seeds and a "Service Garden" (oak = community service, cherry blossom = arts, willow = environment; sprout at 10 badges → sapling at 100 → full tree at 500), rare "shiny" tree mutations, small sponsor discounts. Teammate on the application: Le Phuoc. SDG-aligned (4, 11, 12). Pitched with a Q1 2026 three-school pilot plan; competitors framed as GivePulse, DoSomething.org, SignUpGenius.

**The critique that forced the pivot (recorded in the same doc):** friction (discovery + verification), not motivation, is the real barrier; charging nonprofits to receive volunteers is unethical; consumer-discount financials don't add up; incumbents already do hour-logging; gamification alone gives only a temporary drive (Boy Scouts already proved the model's limits). Recommended pivot: "Volunteer Operations + Verification SaaS with an engagement layer" — sell operational pain relief to institutions, keep gamification as the retention hook.

**Version 2 (by June 2026):** exactly that pivot, matured — verified badges as career credentials, the Sycamore Index, B2B pricing for nonprofits/schools/corporations, College Board positioning. See [[sycamore]] and [[2026-07-07_sycamore-positioning-standardized-volunteering]].

**Lesson:** the idea survived by demoting its most-loved feature (the collectible garden) to a supporting layer and promoting the boring part (verification and admin relief) to the core product.
