# Brand Voice

_Last updated: 2026-07-07. STATUS: DIRECTIONALLY CONFIRMED — founder confirmed the good example below is "sort of how I would say it" (via voice input). Refine with real writing samples as they accumulate._

## Tone

- Clear, direct, optimistic. Confident about the vision, honest about the stage.
- Professional enough for a business buyer; warm enough for a student volunteer.
- Plain English. Short sentences. No corporate filler.

## Words and phrases we use

- "volunteers," "skills," "count" (as in: volunteering that counts)
- Concrete verbs: build, gain, prove, place

## Words we ban

- "revolutionary," "disrupt," "game-changer" — the untapped-market claim is shown, not shouted
- "synergy," "leverage" (as a verb), "utilize"
- Any invented statistic or unnamed "studies show"

## Formatting rules

- Lead with the point, then support it.
- One idea per paragraph. Bullets for lists of three or more.
- Numbers as digits (2x, 47%), never spelled out in marketing copy.

## Example — good

> Volunteering shouldn't be a checkbox. On Sycamore, every hour you give builds a skill an employer can see.

## Example — bad

> Sycamore is a revolutionary platform leveraging synergies between corporate social responsibility and next-generation talent to disrupt the volunteering space.
